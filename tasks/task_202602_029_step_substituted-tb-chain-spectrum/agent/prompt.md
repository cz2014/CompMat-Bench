# Spin-resolved spectrum of a substituted quasi-one-dimensional tight-binding chain

Build the tight-binding model specified below and report how its spin-resolved
spectrum responds to three independent single-parameter changes.

The model is a single-orbital tight-binding chain running along the
c axis of a hexagonal lattice. It has eight orbitals in its repeat unit: four on
magnetic sites and four effective orbitals on non-magnetic triangles. Two spin
blocks (up and down) are built from the same hoppings and differ only through the
sign of an on-site exchange term. Everything needed to construct it is pinned
here; none of it can be derived without the values given.

## Lattice

Hexagonal, with in-plane lattice constant $a = 4.1429$ A and axial constant
$c = 6.7031$ A. Use the standard hexagonal primitive vectors

$$\mathbf a_1 = (a,\ 0,\ 0),\qquad \mathbf a_2 = \left(-\tfrac{a}{2},\ \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a,\ 0\right),\qquad \mathbf a_3 = (0,\ 0,\ c).$$

The chain's periodic images are generated by translation by $c$ along $z$.
This is a model convention, not a crystallographic statement: the decoration
of the eight orbitals below does not repeat under $c$, but every Bloch phase
and every periodic image uses the translation $c$. Reciprocal vectors
$(\mathbf b_1, \mathbf b_2, \mathbf b_3)$ are defined by
$\mathbf b_i \cdot \mathbf a_j = 2\pi\,\delta_{ij}$; fractional
k-coordinates below are expressed in that reciprocal basis.

## The eight orbitals

Along one repeat unit (heights measured in units of c from a reference plane):

- Four magnetic sites on the c axis, carrying one d_xy orbital each:
  M1 at height 0, M2 at c/2, M3 at c, M4 at 3c/2.
  M1 and M3 belong to magnetic sublattice 1; M2 and M4 to magnetic sublattice 2.
- Four non-magnetic triangles, each replaced by one effective p_x orbital
  (defined below): T1 at height c/4, T2 at 3c/4, T3 at 5c/4, T4 at 7c/4.
  Each triangle is an equilateral set of three vertices centered on the c axis
  at in-plane radius $a/\sqrt{3}$, lying in the plane of that triangle's height:
  - T1 and T3 have vertices at azimuths 90, 210, 330 degrees;
  - T2 and T4 have vertices at azimuths 30, 150, 270 degrees;
  azimuth measured counterclockwise from the $\mathbf a_1$ ($+x$) axis toward $+y$.
  T1 and T2 are "substituted" triangles: on each, exactly one vertex is a
  substituent site, the rest ordinary. On T1 the substituent vertex is the one
  at azimuth 330 degrees; on T2 it is the one at azimuth 30 degrees. T3 and T4
  are ordinary (no substituent vertex).

Order the eight orbitals as M1, M2, M3, M4, T1, T2, T3, T4.

## Effective triangle orbital

Each triangle's effective $p_x$ orbital is $1/\sqrt{3}$ times the symmetric sum of the
$p_x$ orbitals on its three vertices. In consequence:

- a matrix element between a magnetic d_xy orbital and a triangle's effective
  orbital is $1/\sqrt{3}$ times the sum, over that triangle's three vertices, of the
  magnetic-site-to-vertex contribution;
- a matrix element between two triangles' effective orbitals is $1/3$ times the sum,
  over the pairs of one vertex from each triangle, of the vertex-to-vertex
  contribution.

Each vertex-level contribution is a two-center bond amplitude (below) times the
Bloch phase $e^{\,i\,\mathbf k \cdot \mathbf R}$, where $\mathbf R$ is the bond
vector between the two sites. For the matrix element in row $i$, column $j$ of
the ordering above, $\mathbf R$ points from the site of orbital $i$ to the site
of orbital $j$; for a magnetic-row element that is from the magnetic site to
the triangle vertex. Bonds are taken to periodic images along the chain
(translations of the partner by integer multiples of $c$). Include every
vertex-level bond whose length is below 4.4 A, counting every image separately
even where two images of the same partner triangle fall inside that cutoff;
longer bonds are dropped.

## Two-center bond amplitudes

For a bond with direction cosines $(l, m, n) = \mathbf R/|\mathbf R|$ (the $x$,
$y$, $z$ components of the unit vector along the bond vector $\mathbf R$) and
two-center parameters $(t_\sigma, t_\pi)$:

- between a $d_{xy}$ orbital and a $p_x$ orbital:
  $$\sqrt{3}\, l^2 m\, t_\sigma + m\,(1 - l^2)\, t_\pi$$
- between two $p_x$ orbitals:
  $$l^2\, t_\sigma + (1 - l^2)\, t_\pi$$

## On-site energies

Every magnetic site shares the on-site energy $\alpha$, shifted by a spin-dependent
exchange term. In the up-spin block, sublattice-1 sites take $\alpha - D/2$ and
sublattice-2 sites take $\alpha + D'/2$, where $D$ is the exchange amplitude assigned
to sublattice 1 and $D'$ the one assigned to sublattice 2. The down-spin block
reverses the sign of both exchange shifts (sublattice-1 sites take $\alpha + D/2$,
sublattice-2 sites take $\alpha - D'/2$). The two blocks are otherwise identical.

Each triangle's effective orbital carries an on-site energy: $\beta_1$ on the
substituted triangles T1 and T2, $\beta_2$ on the ordinary triangles T3 and T4.

Each spin block is the $8 \times 8$ Hermitian matrix with these on-site energies on the
diagonal and the bond matrix elements off the diagonal.

## Bonds and species-dependent hoppings

Only orbitals that are adjacent in one of the two rings below are bonded; all
other pairs have zero matrix element.

- Backbone ring (magnetic-to-triangle): M1-T1-M2-T2-M3-T3-M4-T4-M1. Each
  consecutive pair in this cycle is a bond (so each magnetic site bonds to the two
  triangles flanking it along the chain).
- Triangle ring (triangle-to-triangle): T1-T2-T3-T4-T1. Each consecutive pair is a
  bond.

The two-center parameters $(t_\sigma, t_\pi)$ used for a bond depend on the
substituent character of the vertices involved:

- For a magnetic-to-triangle bond, each vertex contribution uses the
  magnetic-to-substituent parameters if that vertex is a substituent vertex,
  otherwise the magnetic-to-ordinary parameters.
- For a triangle-to-triangle bond, each vertex-pair contribution uses the
  substituent-substituent parameters if both endpoints are substituent vertices,
  the ordinary-substituent parameters if exactly one is, and the
  ordinary-ordinary parameters if neither is.

## Parameter sets

Four parameter sets. All energies in eV.

base:
- $\alpha = -1.85$
- $D = 2.0$, $D' = 2.0$
- $\beta_1 = -4.5$, $\beta_2 = -4.5$
- magnetic-to-ordinary $(t_\sigma, t_\pi) = (-1.42, 0.42)$
- magnetic-to-substituent $(t_\sigma, t_\pi) = (-1.42, 0.42)$
- ordinary-ordinary $(t_\sigma, t_\pi) = (1.112, -0.325)$
- ordinary-substituent $(t_\sigma, t_\pi) = (1.112, -0.325)$
- substituent-substituent $(t_\sigma, t_\pi) = (1.112, -0.325)$

A: as base, except $\beta_1 = -4.0$.

B: as base, except
- magnetic-to-substituent $(t_\sigma, t_\pi) = (-1.14, 0.34)$
- ordinary-substituent $(t_\sigma, t_\pi) = (0.889, -0.26)$
- substituent-substituent $(t_\sigma, t_\pi) = (0.778, -0.227)$

C: as base, except $D = 2.2$.

## The k-path

A two-segment path in fractional reciprocal coordinates:
$L = (1/2, 0, 1/2)$ to $\Gamma = (0, 0, 0)$ to $L' = (0, 1/2, 1/2)$, piecewise linear
with a kink at $\Gamma$. Sample it with 401 uniformly spaced points running from L
through Gamma to L'.

## What to report

At any k, each spin block gives eight energy eigenvalues; sort them ascending and
pair the up-spin and down-spin lists by index. For a given parameter set define:

- the per-index spin difference at that k = the absolute difference between the
  up-spin and down-spin eigenvalue of the same index;
- the split at that k = the maximum per-index spin difference over the eight
  indices.

Report, in eV:

- `eig_up_gamma_base_eV`: the eight up-spin eigenvalues at Gamma for the base set,
  in ascending order.
- `gamma_split_base_eV`: the split at Gamma for the base set.
- `gamma_split_A_eV`: the split at Gamma for set A.
- `gamma_split_B_eV`: the split at Gamma for set B.
- `gamma_split_C_eV`: the split at Gamma for set C.
- `path_max_split_base_eV`: the maximum of the split over all 401 sampled path
  points, for the base set.

Write a single `answer.json` in the workspace root with exactly this schema:


Report at least six significant digits per value. All fields are graded; numbers
